# Chess Wrapped — Design Philosophy

## What is Chess Wrapped?

Chess Wrapped is **not** a dashboard.

It is **not** a profile viewer.

It is **not** a statistics website.

Chess Wrapped is a short storytelling experience that celebrates a player's chess journey.

The purpose of this project is not to show as many statistics as possible.

The purpose is to make players smile, remember their journey, and create something they genuinely want to save and share.

Every design decision should support that goal.

---

# Product Vision

A player enters their Chess.com username.

Within about 30 seconds, they experience a beautifully animated recap of their chess journey.

At the end, they receive a poster they are proud to save and share.

The experience should feel closer to **Spotify Wrapped** or **GitHub Year in Review** than to a statistics dashboard.

---

# Design Principles

## Emotion over Information

Whenever possible, choose emotion over information.

Do not add statistics simply because they are available.

Every piece of information should help tell a story.

If a statistic does not improve the emotional experience, remove it.

---

## Simplicity over Features

More features do not automatically create a better product.

Focus on:

* pacing
* motion
* presentation
* storytelling

Instead of:

* adding more numbers
* adding more charts
* adding more settings

The best experience is often the simplest one.

---

## Story over Dashboard

Every screen should feel like one page of a story.

The user should never feel like they are browsing analytics.

Each scene should naturally lead to the next.

The experience should have a beginning, a middle, and an ending.

---

## Delight in Small Moments

Small details create memorable experiences.

Examples include:

* numbers counting upward
* gentle fades
* tiny sparkles
* subtle scaling
* smooth transitions

Never rely on large or flashy effects.

A premium experience is built from many small moments.

---

## Calm Interface

Whitespace is important.

Avoid visual noise.

Allow every scene to breathe.

Animations should be smooth and calm.

Nothing should ever feel rushed.

---

# Emotional Journey

The experience should guide the player's emotions.

| Scene              | Emotion                 |
| ------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Welcome            | Curiosity               |
| Games Played       | Achievement             |
| Favorite Game Mode | Recognition             |
| Current Rating     | Pride                   |
| Win Rate           | Reflection              |
| Player Summary     | Celebration             |
| Final Poster       | "I want to share this." |

Every scene exists to make the final poster feel meaningful.

---

# Visual Identity

The visual style should feel:

* Cozy
* Cute
* Soft
* Premium
* Minimal
* Friendly

Cute does **not** mean childish.

Premium does **not** mean luxurious.

Aim for the feeling of a beautifully crafted indie product.

---

# Design Language

Avoid traditional chess aesthetics such as:

* dark wood textures
* black and gold themes
* medieval styling
* realistic chess pieces

Instead, embrace a modern, soft identity.

Primary color:

* Lavender

Supporting colors:

* Cream
* Mint
* White
* Dark Gray

Illustrations should be:

* rounded
* minimal
* friendly
* flat
* immediately recognizable

---

# Motion Philosophy

Motion exists to guide attention.

It should never become the center of attention.

If users notice the animation itself, it is probably too much.

Prefer:

* fade
* translateY
* subtle scale
* count-up
* staggered appearance

Avoid:

* spinning
* dramatic rotations
* excessive bouncing
* flashy particle effects
* long transitions

Motion should feel similar to:

* Apple
* Spotify Wrapped
* GitHub Year in Review

Not:

* mobile games
* gaming UI
* analytics dashboards

---

# Poster Philosophy

The poster is the destination of the entire experience.

Everything before it exists to make the final poster feel valuable.

The poster should feel like:

* a memory
* a postcard
* a collectible
* something worth sharing

It should **not** feel like:

* a profile card
* a statistics report
* an analytics dashboard

When someone sees the poster on social media, they should immediately recognize it as a Wrapped experience.

---

# What We Don't Build

Chess Wrapped is **not** trying to become:

* Chess.com
* Lichess
* an analytics platform
* a coaching tool
* a statistics explorer

Those are different products with different goals.

Chess Wrapped celebrates the player's journey.

---

# Development Mindset

When making design decisions, always ask:

> Does this make the experience more memorable?

If the answer is no,

don't add it.

Always prefer:

* simplicity
* consistency
* atmosphere
* emotion

over:

* complexity
* decoration
* more features
* more information

---

# Final Goal

The best version of Chess Wrapped is **not** the one with the most features.

It is the one players remember.

The user should finish with one simple feeling:

> "That was fun."

And one simple action:

> "I want to share this."
